# **OMIP-­119: A 36-­Color Full-­Spectrum Flow Cytometry Panel** **for Deep Immunophenotyping of Peripheral Blood and** **Ex Vivo Expanded Human T Cells**

**ABSTRACT**
The increasing uptake of adoptive CAR-­T and TCR-­T cell therapies into clinical practice has intensified the need for robust
and detailed immunophenotyping of ex vivo expanded T cells. We have developed and optimized an extended 36-­color full-­
spectrum flow cytometry panel suitable for in-­depth immunophenotyping of both peripheral blood and ex vivo expanded human
T cells. Our panel allows for analysis of CD4 [+] and CD8 [+] memory subpopulations (Tn/Tscm, Tcm, Tem, and Temra), Treg subsets
(CD45RA [+] and CD39 [+] ), and helper T cell subsets (Tfh, Th1, Th2, and Th17). Intracellular staining facilitates the evaluation of
functional markers granzyme B (cytotoxicity) and Ki-­67 (proliferation). Our panel allows detailed investigation of T cell activation using markers carrying a range of expression kinetics. Exhaustion and senescence features, known to correlate with resistance to immunotherapies, can also be thoroughly evaluated. TCR staining with pHLA-­dextramer or anti-­TCR antibody is also
included to allow evaluation of TCR specificity and/or transduction efficiency. A PD-­1 receptor occupancy component enables
quantification of T cells bound to antibodies from immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapies. Overall, we have generated a
fully optimized and fit-­for-­purpose extended T cell profiling panel with particular relevance to the field of immunotherapies
including ICIs and adoptive T cell therapies.



**1  |  Background**


Technological advances in the field of flow cytometry have
pushed beyond conventional approaches limited by measuring
only the peak emission wavelengths toward utilizing the entire
fluorescent spectrum with full-­spectrum flow cytometry. With
an expanding selection of fluorophores covering previously underutilized parts of the spectrum, full-­spectrum panels comprising as many as 50 colors have been developed on specialized
cytometers [1]. Aside from extending the limits on the number of
parameters, full-­spectrum analyses can also deliver lower limits
of detection and higher signal resolution [2].


Full-­spectrum flow cytometry panels assembled for T-­cell immunophenotyping have primarily focused on characterizing the
steady-­state phenotype of T cells derived from peripheral blood




[3, 4]. However, the increasing uptake of adoptive T cell therapies into clinical practice has intensified the need for robust
and detailed immunophenotyping of ex vivo expanded T cells,
especially considering the phenotype of CAR-­T cells can predict
therapeutic response and toxicity [5]. To fill this gap, we have
developed and optimized an extended 36-­color flow cytometry
panel for deep immunophenotyping of both peripheral blood
and ex vivo expanded human T cells using the Cytek Aurora
full-­spectrum flow cytometer (Table 1). The staining reagents
required for the performance of the 36-­color panel are outlined
in Table 2.

An example analysis using the 36-­color panel is illustrated
in Figure 1. Live T cells are identified using FSC/SSC, Live/
Dead Blue and CD3 (Figure 1A). Gating the CD3 [+] population
is performed against CD45RA using a skewed quadrant gate
to exclude potential false positive events arising from spillover
spreading error (Figure S10). Monitoring PD-­1 receptor occupancy can be achieved by comparing staining for the competitive and noncompetitive anti-­PD-­1 clones with an example from
a proof-­of-­concept study using healthy peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) shown in Figure 1B.


To define T cell subpopulations present in either peripheral
blood or ex vivo expanded T cells, CD4 [+], and CD8 [+] T cells are
initially selected from the CD3 [+] population, and their memory
subsets identified based on the expression of CD45RA, CCR7,
CD95, CD28, and CD27 [8–10] (Figure 1C). Within the CD4 [+]
population, regulatory T cells (Tregs) are identified using FoxP3
and CD25 and further characterized by CD45RA to identify
naïve/resting (CD45RA [+] ) Tregs, and the functional marker
CD39 to identify Tregs with increased immunosuppressive capacity [11, 12]. Helper T cell subsets are identified using CXCR3,
CCR4, and CCR6 for Th1, Th2, and Th17 populations [8, 9] and
CXCR5 for follicular helper T cells (Tfh) [13]. Expression of
granzyme B within CD4 [+] cells identifies CD4 cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTL) [14].


The PE channel is designated for T cell receptor (TCR) staining, either by pHLA-­dextramer (Figure S6), or by anti-­TCR
antibody such as commercially available anti-­Vα or -­Vβ clones.
Dextramer staining allows identification of CD4 [+] and CD8 [+]
T cells expressing antigen-­specific TCRs, with expression in



ex vivo expanded T cells shown in Figure 1C. Both pHLA-­
dextramer and anti-­TCR antibody detection can be used to
identify transduced T cells and quantify transduction efficiency
in adoptive T cell therapies.


Due to the complexity of the data produced by this 36-­color
panel, an analysis workflow containing dimensionality reduction and clustering algorithms is important to unlock the full
potential of the data. To demonstrate the applicability of data
generated by this panel in such a workflow, two datasets, one
containing four healthy donor PBMC samples and the other containing four healthy donor expanded T cell samples, were analyzed separately.


Before being subject to automated analysis approaches, data
were put through a clean-­up process to ensure data quality.
Accurate unmixing and scaling of the data were checked before the FlowAI cleaning algorithm [15] was applied to remove
anomalous and artefactual events. Post-­cleaning, CD3 [+] T cells
were selected as the population of interest to be analyzed. An
opt-­SNE [16] was run using the key lineage markers for each
dataset to group together events based on phenotypic similarities. The FlowSOM algorithm [17] was then run using the same
markers and the resulting clusters overlaid onto the opt-­SNE
parameters (Figure 1D). Clusters were identified through a
clustered heatmap analysis (Figure S16) and corresponded with
the expected populations for both the peripheral blood and expanded T cell datasets.


Expression of individual markers associated with T cell activation (CXCR3, CD25, CD38, and HLA-­DR), co-­stimulation
(CD226, 4–1BB, ICOS, and OX40), function (granzyme B and
Ki-­67), exhaustion/inhibition (CD101, CTLA-­4, LAG-­3, PD-­1,
TIGIT, and TIM-­3), senescence (CD57 and KLRG1), and differentiation (CD62L and CD127) within peripheral blood and expanded T cells is illustrated in Figure 1E.


Examination of activation marker expression can provide insights into the dynamics of T cell responses in peripheral blood
or expanded T cell settings, and we have therefore included several T cell activation markers spanning early to late expression
kinetics. HLA-­DR is a late T cell activation marker frequently
used in combination with CD38 to identify activated T cell subsets [9]. A recent study found HLA-­DR [+] CD38 [hi] CD8 [+] T cells
associated with systemic inflammation, tissue injury and immune disorders of severe COVID-­19 patients [18]. TNFR family
members OX40 and 4–1BB are transiently expressed activation
markers absent in peripheral blood T cells but expressed among
expanded T cells [19]. The co-­stimulatory receptor ICOS regulates T cell activation and expression can be detected in peripheral blood T cells, with enhanced expression among expanded
T cells [20]. CD226 is a co-­activating receptor critical for CD8
T cell effector function and its axis includes competition with
inhibitory receptors such as TIGIT [21]. Reduced expression has
been linked to curbed effector CD8 T cell responses which could
limit the efficacy of ICIs [22].


Our panel enables functional T cell characterization via intracellular staining. Granzyme B is a marker of cytotoxic function
expressed highly in CD8 T cell subsets and CD4 CTLs [14].

**2  |  Similarity to Other OMIPs**


Our panel builds upon the 31-­color full-­spectrum flow cytometry panel OMIP-­099 with 24 shared markers, 14 of which
carry the same fluorophore. Our panel also shares substantial homology (24 markers) with the 36-­color full-­spectrum
panel OMIP-­114. These panels were designed for in-­depth T
cell immunophenotyping within human PBMC (OMIP-­099)
and whole blood (OMIP-­114). To date, no OMIPs have been
published that are dually optimized for both peripheral blood
and ex vivo expanded T cells. In addition, no published OMIPs
have included an RO component such as the PD-­1 RO assay
included in our panel. Other human T cell focused OMIPs
using full-­spectrum flow cytometry with > 10 shared markers
include OMIP-­060, OMIP-­067, OMIP-­071, OMIP-­075, OMIP-­
084, OMIP-­091, OMIP-­096, and OMIP-­116. None of the similar
OMIPs include the marker CD101, an emerging T cell exhaustion marker shown to correlate with progression following
CAR-­T cell therapy [5].
